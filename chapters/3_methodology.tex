\section{System Overview}

\QFlow\ is placed between control-plane candidate generation and final policy
validation. The KMS/SDN controller obtains authenticated network metadata and
generates admissible candidate routes. The accelerator encodes the current state,
selects one scoring profile, evaluates the supplied candidates, and returns a
candidate identifier, score, profile identifier, and status. The controller remains
responsible for security checks, fallback, and route installation.

\section{System Boundary}

The accelerator receives fixed-width descriptors and bounded controller features.
It does not receive secret-key bits, discover network topology, generate arbitrary
paths, operate the KMS, update an RL Q-table, or install a route. Its output is a
recommendation within a deterministic arithmetic contract. This boundary is applied
consistently to software, RTL, post-route, and physical measurements.

\section{Network and Key-Resource Model}

The trusted-relay network is a directed graph $G=(V,E)$. At decision epoch $n$, link
$(i,j)$ is represented by
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{ij}[n]
=\left(K_{ij}[n],K_{ij}^{\max},\lambda_{ij}[n],C_{ij}[n],q_{ij}[n],
\mathrm{status}_{ij}[n],u_{ij}[n],\mathcal{M}_{ij}[n]\right),
\label{eq:method-link-state}
\end{equation}
where $K_{ij}$ is available classical key material, $\lambda_{ij}$ is effective
key-generation rate, $C_{ij}$ is consumption, $q_{ij}$ is a QBER-derived or other
external health indicator, $u_{ij}$ is utilization, and $\mathcal{M}_{ij}$ contains
authenticated policy metadata. The pool follows the balance in
\eqref{eq:key-pool-balance}.

For a demand $D[n]$, hard eligibility is
\begin{equation}
\chi_{ij}[n;D]
=\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{status}_{ij}=\mathrm{UP}\}
 \mathbf{1}\{K_{ij}[n]\geq D[n]\}
 \mathbf{1}\{q_{ij}[n]\leq q_{\mathrm{abort}}\}
 \mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{M}_{ij}[n]\ \text{is valid}\}.
\label{eq:method-link-feasible}
\end{equation}
Policy metadata may encode authorization, provenance, expiry, revocation, or an
alarm. Optional freshness is a KMS/security rule. It is not modelled as quantum
decoherence of stored classical keys.

\section{Candidate-Route Representation}

For each request, software supplies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{sd}
=\{\mathcal{R}_0,\mathcal{R}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{m-1}\}.
\label{eq:method-candidate-set}
\end{equation}
Candidate $\mathcal{R}_i$ is described by
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{c}_i=
\left(v_i,\widehat d_i,\widehat o_i,\widehat r_i,
\widehat u_i,\widehat\rho_i,h_i,\mathrm{id}_i\right),
\label{eq:candidate-vector}
\end{equation}
where $v_i$ is feasibility; $\widehat d_i$ is normalized distance or consumption
cost; $\widehat o_i$ is bottleneck occupancy; $\widehat r_i$ is replenishment or
effective key-generation condition; $\widehat u_i$ is utilization or imbalance;
$\widehat\rho_i$ is bounded operational risk; $h_i$ is hop count; and
$\mathrm{id}_i$ is the deterministic tie-break identifier. Hatted fields are
dimensionless and saturated to their declared ranges.

\section{Eligibility Rules}

Candidate feasibility is computed before scoring:
\begin{equation}
v_i[n]=\prod_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{R}_i}\chi_{uv}[n;D].
\label{eq:method-route-feasible}
\end{equation}
An infeasible candidate cannot win through soft weights. If no candidate is
feasible, the kernel returns a no-path status. Invalid encodings return an invalid
status. Exact status behavior and request timing are specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-point}.

\section{Fixed-Point Candidate-Scoring Model}

For candidate $\mathcal{R}_i$, the normalized soft-penalty vector is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_i[n]
=\begin{bmatrix}
1-\widehat o_i[n]\\
1-\widehat r_i[n]\\
\widehat u_i[n]\\
\widehat\rho_i[n]
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:method-penalties}
\end{equation}
For active profile $\Pi_k$, the saturated local cost is
\begin{equation}
c_i^{(k)}[n]
=\operatorname{sat}_{W_c}
\left(\sum_{r=1}^{4}\alpha_r^{(k)}\phi_{i,r}[n]\right).
\label{eq:method-profile-cost}
\end{equation}
The first path objective is the normalized local cost; the remaining objectives
represent bottleneck reserve and utilization balance:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\boldsymbol f}_i^{(k)}[n]
=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal{N}_{c}\!\left(c_i^{(k)}[n]\right)\\
1-o_{\min}(\mathcal{R}_i,n)\\
\widehat I(\mathcal{R}_i,n)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:method-objectives}
\end{equation}
The Tchebycheff score is
\begin{equation}
g_i^{(k)}[n]
=\max_{j\in\{1,2,3\}}
\left\{\lambda_{\mathrm{TCH},j}^{(k)}
\left|\widehat f_{i,j}^{(k)}[n]-z_j^{\star}\right|\right\}.
\label{eq:method-tchebycheff}
\end{equation}
Among feasible candidates, the minimum score wins; an exact tie is resolved by the
smallest candidate identifier. Scores are compared only within the active profile
and fixed numeric contract.

\section{Controller State Representation}

The adaptive observation is
\begin{equation}
\bm{x}[n]=\left(x_B[n],x_R[n],x_L[n],x_I[n]\right),
\label{eq:state-features}
\end{equation}
where $x_B$ is minimum normalized candidate key-pool occupancy, $x_R$ is normalized
replenishment/effective key-generation condition, $x_L$ is offered request load,
and $x_I$ is utilization imbalance. Each feature is clipped and assigned to one of
four bins using three fixed thresholds. Equality enters the higher bin.

The bins are packed in radix four:
\begin{equation}
z[n]=\left((4b_B+b_R)4+b_L\right)4+b_I,
\qquad z[n]\in\{0,\ldots,255\}.
\label{eq:state-pack}
\end{equation}
Four bins for each of four features produce $4^4=256$ states. The action
$a[n]\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ selects profile $\Pi_{a[n]}$.

\section{Offline Policy Learning}

Eight tabular Q-learning candidates used trainer seeds 17, 29, 43, 71, 113, 167,
229, and 283. Each candidate trained for 200 epochs and 819,200 transitions. The
learning rate was $\eta_{\mathrm{RL}}=0.1$, the discount factor was
$\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}=0.95$, and exploration decreased linearly from 1.0 to 0.05
over the first 80\% of training transitions.

Training, validation, and test traces were separated. Candidate eligibility
required at least two used profiles, at least 1\% use of the second-most-used
profile, blocking within an absolute 0.005 margin, and validation switch rate no
greater than 0.25. Eligible candidates were ranked by validation reward, blocking,
quality outcome, switching, and trainer seed. Seed 229 was selected and its policy,
semantic description, and file hashes were fixed before held-out evaluation.

\begin{pseudocode}{Pseudocode 3.1. Offline training, selection, and policy freeze}
\textbf{Input:} training traces $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, validation traces
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$, and trainer seeds $\mathcal{S}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} frozen policy $\pi_{229}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Initialize one tabular Q-function for every seed in $\mathcal{S}$.
  \item Apply Q-learning updates over the training traces for 200 epochs.
  \item Evaluate each final policy on validation traces and record decomposed
  reward, blocking, profile use, and switching.
  \item Apply the declared eligibility rules and rank the eligible policies.
  \item Select seed 229; record configuration and file hashes.
  \item Lock the policy before accessing the held-out traces.
\end{enumerate}
\end{pseudocode}

\section{Policy and Profile ROM Deployment}

The deployed policy ROM contains 256 state-major two-bit actions. The profile ROM
contains four 18-bit words. A 1,024-word signed Q8.7 table was retained for training
analysis but is not read by the deployed controller. Replacing runtime argmax over
four Q-values with the frozen two-bit action removes unnecessary storage and
comparison logic while preserving the selected policy.

\section{Minimum-Dwell Mechanism}

Let $a^{\star}[n]=\pi(z[n])$ be the requested policy action and $\delta[n]$ the
number of accepted decisions since the last profile change. The deployed action is
\begin{equation}
a[n]=
\begin{cases}
a[n-1], & a^{\star}[n]\neq a[n-1]\ \land\ \delta[n]<d_{\min},\\
a^{\star}[n], & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:dwell}
\end{equation}
with $d_{\min}=3$. Dwell is counted in accepted decisions, not idle clock cycles.
The mechanism is evaluated as part of the complete configuration H4 controller; the
experiment does not isolate policy and dwell contributions.

\section{Fixed-Point Representation}

The reference and RTL use explicit width, signedness, scaling, rounding, saturation,
packing order, and tie-breaking rules. The four-profile payload contains 72 useful
bits in total, and the action uses two useful bits. State IDs and output fields are
compared as exact integers rather than floating-point approximations. Appendix
\ref{app:fixed-point} records the storage and protocol layout.

\section{Verification Methodology}

Verification proceeded from the mathematical reference to the physical board:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item fixed-point software reference checks;
  \item exhaustive state encoder, policy ROM, and profile ROM tests;
  \item cycle-accurate unit and integrated RTL simulation;
  \item independent synthesis, mapping, placement, routing, and timing for every
  hardware configuration; and
  \item physical UART replay with field-by-field comparison.
\end{enumerate}

Controller verification comprised 1,509 cycle vectors and 302 valid decisions,
including all 256 states, 36 threshold-edge cases, all four actions, dwell holds and
switches, invalid input, no path, reset, pipeline wait, and busy behavior. Seventy-four
Python regression tests passed. Integrated tracing added 2,125 cycle vectors and
530 valid decisions. Evidence type was recorded separately for software, RTL,
post-synthesis, post-route, and physical-board results.

\section{FPGA Implementation}

The target was a Digilent Nexys 3 Rev~B with a Xilinx XC6SLX16-2-CSG324 Spartan-6
FPGA. Xilinx ISE 14.7 independently built configurations H0--H4 with the same board
shell, UART protocol, 100~MHz user clock, candidate widths, output schema, and replay
rows. The 100~MHz value is the operating contract; maximum frequency was extracted
from each post-route timing report.

\section{Physical Replay Protocol}

For each configuration, the host programmed the corresponding bitstream, transmitted
one fixed record at a time, captured the raw UART response, parsed the record, and
compared nine output fields with the reference. Eighty-four records produced 756
checked fields per configuration and 3,780 across the five configurations.

\begin{pseudocode}{Pseudocode 3.2. Physical replay and exact comparison}
\textbf{Input:} configuration $h$, replay rows $\mathcal{D}$, and expected results
$Y_h$.\\
\textbf{Output:} raw capture, parsed records, and exactness summary.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Program the independently generated bitstream for configuration $h$.
  \item Transmit each encoded replay row and retain the raw response.
  \item Parse result, status, profile, score, identifier, and cycle fields.
  \item Compare all nine checked fields with $Y_h$.
  \item Record row count, exact fields, mismatches, and protocol status.
  \item Hash the capture, parsed data, implementation reports, and summary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{pseudocode}

Kernel latency for record $i$ is
\begin{equation}
L_i=c_{\mathrm{done},i}-c_{\mathrm{accept},i},
\qquad t_i=L_iT_{\mathrm{clk}}.
\label{eq:latency}
\end{equation}
The boundary excludes UART serialization, host parsing, operating-system scheduling,
JTAG programming, KMS acquisition, candidate generation, security validation, and
route installation.

\section{Statistical Analysis}

Exactness required equality of every checked integer field. Timing and resource
metrics came directly from independent implementation reports. Adaptive behavior
was summarized over valid decisions by profile counts, transitions, dwell runs, and
dwell lengths.

For held-out route quality, only jointly successful pairs were compared. The analysis
reported pair count, paired mean difference, bootstrap 95\% confidence interval,
two-sided sign-test $p$-value, paired effect size $d_z$, and path-change count. The
limited number of distinct replay rows and repeated structure can reduce statistical
power and independence. Scores formed under different active profiles were not
treated as directly comparable physical units.

\section{Supporting VLSI Methodology}
\label{sec:vlsi-method}

The supporting VLSI study used the version-controlled evidence snapshot identified
in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}. FDPE-V0--V3, SKAG-W0--W1, and
Pareto-C0--C1 were synthesized with Yosys, and generic cell counts were read from
the version-controlled synthesis logs. Generic cells are used only for within-flow
structural comparisons; they are not FPGA LUTs.

Representative kernels SKAG-W1, FDPE-V3, and Pareto-C0 were separately processed
through a SKY130 high-density OpenROAD flow comprising synthesis, floorplanning,
placement, clock-tree synthesis, routing, parasitic extraction, and final report
generation. Their areas refer to isolated routed kernels, not an integrated \QFlow\
ASIC. FDPE-V3 retains its repository identifier as a supplementary arithmetic
interpolation experiment and is not presented as current candidate-evaluator
functionality.

A representative 2:1 transmission-gate multiplexer was also simulated in ngspice
with simplified Level-1 MOS models at 1.8~V and loads of 5, 10, 20, and 50~fF. The
calculated $\tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$ quantity describes only the stated capacitive load, not
accelerator energy per decision.

\section{Methodological Limitations}

The physical replay used one board and device family, 84 rows per configuration,
and a bounded candidate interface. The learned policy was trained on a controlled
research environment and was not evaluated across a large set of unseen production
topologies. No matched CPU timing experiment or activity-calibrated FPGA power
measurement was completed. Live QKD devices, KMS/SDN orchestration, authenticated
host transport, and route installation were outside the experiment. The VLSI work
is isolated-kernel physical-design evidence rather than full-chip signoff.

\chapterconclusion{%
The methodology combines a classical key-resource model, explicit eligibility,
profile-conditioned fixed-point scoring, four-feature state encoding, offline policy
learning, deterministic ROM inference, and independent same-board implementation.
Its verification and statistical procedures distinguish exact hardware correctness
from network-level route-quality evidence.}
