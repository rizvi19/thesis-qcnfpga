\section{Reward Scale and Policy Storage Detail}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Evaluation reward extrema by outcome.}
\label{tab:reward-extrema}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.22\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Outcome} & \textbf{Expression} & \textbf{Interpretation}\\
\midrule
Blocked, no switch & $-4$ & Blocking term only\\
Blocked, switch & $-4.25$ & Blocking plus profile-change penalty\\
Successful & $r_t^{\mathrm{eval}}=4+2U_Q+U_B-0.25h-0.25\sigma$ & Bounded quality/balance utility
offset by hop and switching costs\\
Declared maximum & $6.75$ & Nominal best successful combination under the
registered contract\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:reward-extrema} reports the common evaluation reward. The successful
training reward replaces $U_B$ with $4U_B$; the blocked rule remains
$r_t^{\mathrm{train}}=-4-0.25\sigma_t$ because its balance utility is zero.

The deployed policy requires 512 useful bits ($256\times2$). The profile ROM adds
72 useful bits ($4\times18$). By comparison, the archival Q-table contains 16,384
bits ($1024\times16$). Exporting the greedy actions therefore removes the runtime
Q-value maximum and greatly reduces useful policy storage, while preserving one
deterministic action for every quantized state.
